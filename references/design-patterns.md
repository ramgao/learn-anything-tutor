# Experimental design patterns

These patterns are original combinations and operating rules for this skill. They have not been validated as a package or shown superior to other tutors. Measure actual learning before making effectiveness claims.

## Frontier map

Write an internal three-part map: **target capability → current blocking step → next observable task**. Update it after each learner attempt. If the learner's answer changes the diagnosis, revise the map rather than continuing a preset lesson. Show it to the learner only when a plan would help.

Example: target = explain an HTTP request; block = confusing a header with a request body; next task = label those parts in a short captured request.

Failure check: Do not infer a deficit from one ambiguous response. Ask a discriminating question or offer a short explanation.

## Evidence ladder

Use four levels when deciding what to teach next:

1. **Familiarity:** recognizes a term or example.
2. **Guided performance:** succeeds with hints or a model.
3. **Independent performance:** succeeds without hints on a comparable task.
4. **Transfer:** applies the idea to a meaningfully different task and explains why it fits.

The levels are a decision aid, not a psychometric scale. A single success is limited evidence; invite later retrieval when retention matters.

## Struggle dial

Start with a task the learner can plausibly attempt. After an unsuccessful attempt, choose one of: clarify the question, give a small cue, expose a worked step, or supply the full answer. Move the dial toward more help when the learner asks, time is short, or repeated attempts add no insight. Move it toward less help after success. Do not use a fixed number of mandatory hints.

## Source-to-skill bridge

When research is needed: verify the source and its date; extract the exact claim and limits; explain it in the learner's language; then ask for an application, comparison, or decision based on the claim. A citation alone does not establish understanding. Mark uncertain or disputed points.

## Error fingerprint

Describe a repeated error as an observable rule, such as “assumes all HTTP 403 responses mean authentication failed,” rather than a trait such as “bad at web security.” Give a pair of examples where the wrong rule and correct rule lead to different conclusions. Ask the learner to choose and explain. Retire the fingerprint once independent work no longer shows it.

## How to evaluate the patterns

Compare learner performance on a new task before and after tutoring, then check again after a delay if possible. Note hints used, time, and transfer quality. Compare with a simpler baseline where practical. Collect learner feedback about pace and clarity. Revise patterns based on observed failures, not preference alone.
